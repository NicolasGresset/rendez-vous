Source code

For transparency, the whole tool is open source: github.com/NicolasGresset/rendez-vous. The code is under the MIT licence, the places database under the ODbL. Everything described below can be checked there: what the page sends, and to whom.

Privacy in short

  • No account, no cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising.
  • The computation runs on the author's server, which keeps nothing you type or choose once it has answered; only your IP address stays in its memory, for at most 24 hours, to limit the number of computations per visitor.
  • Your browser contacts other services to get addresses, maps and travel times, listed below with what they receive.

Hosting

Vercel serves the pages. Like any web server, it receives your IP address, the address of the page requested, your browser's description (user agent) and the page you came from, and keeps them in its logs to run and protect the service, under its privacy policy. Vercel is in the United States and relies on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. The fonts, the map library and the places database are served by the tool itself, not by third parties.

Third-party services contacted by your browser

Each of these services receives, like any website, your IP address and your browser's description, plus what is listed, and processes them under its own privacy policy.

  • OpenStreetMap (OpenStreetMap Foundation, United Kingdom), tile.openstreetmap.org: the map images, hence the area you look at.
  • IGN Géoplateforme (Institut national de l'information géographique et forestière, France), data.geopf.fr: what you type in an address field (from 3 characters), and your position when you click « ma position actuelle », to find its address.

The times and journeys are computed by the author's own server, api.rendez-vous.gresset-bourgeois.fr, in France: it receives the positions of the participants, their modes of transport and numbers of people, the place type, the meeting time, and, like any server, your IP address. The connection is encrypted (HTTPS). The positions are used for the computation only: neither they nor your IP address are written to any log or kept after the answer. Your IP address is held in memory only, for at most 24 hours, to limit the number of computations per visitor. No third party receives these positions.

The addresses you enter may be other people's: they go to the services above as described, so a nearby landmark (a metro station, a square) works as well as an exact home address. The OpenStreetMap and Google Maps links of a proposed place send nothing until you follow them; « Partager le lieu » hands the place's name, address and OpenStreetMap link to your device's share menu or clipboard, not to this site. Links to other sites (GitHub, the author's site…) send nothing until you follow them.

Cookies and storage

The tool sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser, so there is no consent banner. Your position is only read when you click « ma position actuelle », after your browser asks your permission.

Your rights

These transfers rest on the legitimate interest of running the tool and providing the features you use (article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR). The GDPR gives you rights over data about you (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection); here is where such data actually is:

  • With me: nothing, unless you write to me. The tool has no database and no accounts, and the server's logs hold neither IP addresses nor positions. If you send me an email, I keep it as long as our exchange needs it, and delete it if you ask.
  • With the host: Vercel's logs, kept and deleted under its own policy. I cannot read them by visitor; you can write to Vercel at privacy@vercel.com, or ask me to pass your request on.
  • With the third-party services listed above: each answers for what it receives, under its own privacy policy; write to it directly.

Contact: nicolas@gresset-bourgeois.fr. You may also lodge a complaint with the French data protection authority, the CNIL.
